Overview

The Challenge

Movix is a peer-to-peer delivery platform that needed to serve two fundamentally different users simultaneously: the sender, who needs to trust their package is safe, and the driver, who needs clarity, efficiency, and confidence while on the road.

Peer-to-peer is faster and cheaper, but both sides are strangers.

Trust is the product.

Decide

Five decisions that turn worry into proof.

Key design decisions

Five decisions that rebuilt trust.

Each one starts from a research insight and names what it costs.

01 — Sign-up

Sign up with a phone number, not a form.

Insight:

Sign-up felt overwhelming before anyone had sent a thing.

Options:

A long registration form vs phone + code.

Decision:

Phone + SMS code + optional passkey; details are collected at the first booking.

Trade-off:

Less data upfront.

Left: one field to start, no password to invent. Right: the SMS code doubles as verification, so there’s no email step.

02 — Pricing

Drivers make offers, senders choose.

Insight:

“Is the price fair?” came up at every booking.

Options:

A fixed price set by the app vs driver offers.

Decision:

Jane sets a max price, drivers offer under it, and she compares price, rating and pickup time.

Trade-off:

A short wait for offers, shown as “usually 2–5 min”.

Left: typical offers are shown so the max price isn’t a guess. Right: Liam’s $25 offer next to rating and pickup time; Jane pays $29.50.

03 — Proof

Every delivery ends with proof.

Insight:

“Delivered” felt like a guess.

Decision:

A delivery photo + a 4-digit code from the recipient close every job.

Trade-off:

One extra step for the driver.

Left: Liam can’t finish without the photo and Olivia’s code 7305. Right: Jane sees the photo and 3:38 PM, so “delivered” is a fact.

04 — Chat

Quick replies when the driver is driving.

Insight:

Drivers can’t type while driving. Uber found the same and built one-tap replies (source: [LINK]).

Decision:

A driving mode with large quick replies; full chat when parked.

Trade-off:

Fewer things Liam can say while moving.

Left: big targets Liam can hit at a red light, not a keyboard. Right: the delivery card pins the context, so nobody asks “which order?”

05 — Approval

Approval you can see.

Insight:

“How long will approval take?”

Decision:

A 3-step sign-up with a documents checklist and a review status with a timeline.

Trade-off:

More screens, but each one is short.

Left: Liam sees exactly what’s left and can stop and come back. Right: waiting has a timeline instead of silence.

Reflection

What I learned.

01

What worked

  • Designing from the journey maps.Every key screen answers a real pain point: offers for “Is the price fair?”, live tracking for “Where’s my package?”, photo proof for “‘Delivered’ felt like a guess”.
  • Looking at how others solve it.Studying Uber, DoorDash and Lalamove shaped the offers model, the driver documents checklist and the proof-of-delivery step.
  • Designing both sides as one system.Mapping where sender and driver meet (offer, tracking and chat, proof) kept the two apps connected rather than separate products.
02

What I’d do differently

  • Set up the “story data” on day one.Names, prices and addresses drifted between sections, and fixing them late cost time. I’d define one persona, one delivery and one design system before the first screen.
  • Design the unhappy paths earlier.I started with the happy path and added “no offers”, “wrong code” and “cancel” later. Starting with them would have changed some layouts sooner.
  • Test before polishing.I went to high fidelity before putting the flows in front of users. Testing with a few people on wireframes first would have caught problems more cheaply.
03

Next steps

  • Usability testing, round 1:5 senders + 5 drivers on the Figma prototype, using the 4 tasks from the test plan.
  • The missing pieces:a tracking page for the recipient, and a flow for reporting a damaged or missing package.
  • An accessibility check:colour contrast, larger text sizes and VoiceOver labels, especially for drivers reading at a glance.
